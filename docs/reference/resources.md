# Free resources

The best free material to pair with each phase. Videos are good for intuition; courses and books are for depth and many more exercises. Everything here is free to access.

## MIT OpenCourseWare

| Phase | Course | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 9 | [6.042J Mathematics for Computer Science](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2015/) | proofs, induction, counting, graphs, number theory, asymptotics |
| 2 | [18.01SC Single Variable Calculus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/) | full calculus course with recitations and problem sets |
| 3 | [18.06SC Linear Algebra](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/) | Gilbert Strang's legendary course, the template for Phase 3 |

| 5 | [6.041SC Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-041sc-probabilistic-systems-analysis-and-applied-probability-fall-2013/) | deeper probability, with John Tsitsiklis |

## Videos for intuition

- **3Blue1Brown**, [Essence of Linear Algebra](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/linear-algebra) and [Essence of Calculus](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/calculus). Pairs with Phases 2–3. Watch before or alongside each lesson.
- **3Blue1Brown**, [Neural networks](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/neural-networks): backpropagation visualized, for after [the chain rule lesson](../multivariable/chain-rule.md).
- **StatQuest** (Josh Starmer) on YouTube: friendly explanations of statistics and ML concepts.

## Free books

- **Mathematics for Machine Learning**, Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong ([mml-book.github.io](https://mml-book.github.io/)): the closest single book to this site's ML track.
- **Convex Optimization**, Boyd & Vandenberghe ([web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/)): the standard reference for Phase 7.
- **Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms**, David MacKay ([inference.org.uk/itila](https://www.inference.org.uk/itila/)): beautiful and intuitive, for Phase 8.
- **Introduction to Probability**, Blitzstein & Hwang ([Stat 110 course site](https://www.stat110.net/)): the book behind Harvard's Stat 110, with lectures on YouTube.
- **Linear Algebra Done Right**, Sheldon Axler ([linear.axler.net](https://linear.axler.net/)): a more abstract, proof-centered second pass on linear algebra.
- **Deep Learning**, Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville ([deeplearningbook.org](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/)): Part I is a compact math review; Part II applies it.

## Using AI alongside this site

Good prompts once you've attempted something yourself:

- *"Here's my proof of X. Check every step and tell me which one is weakest."*
- *"Give me three more exercises like this one, at increasing difficulty, with solutions I can reveal one at a time."*
- *"Explain [concept] using a physical analogy, then a geometric one, then a computational one."*
- *"I understand A and B. Show me how C connects them."*
- *"Write a short Python/NumPy script that demonstrates [theorem] numerically."* Verifying a theorem numerically is a great way to make it real.
